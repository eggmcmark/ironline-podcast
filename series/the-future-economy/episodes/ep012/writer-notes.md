# Writer Notes — Episode 12: "Salt Owed"

## What I Was Going For

The episode had one structural demand: the junction had to fail during it, not before or after. Ep 11's writer flagged this — the acceleration model put failure at four to five days from the second way-station departure, which meant it would land mid-episode. I built the episode around that event arriving via relay post in the managed corridor rather than being witnessed directly. This keeps it operational (it's news she receives, not a thing she sees), which felt right — Kirin has never seen the junction fail, only tracked its decline.

The title "Salt Owed" carries two meanings: the literal debt Naila has been carrying since episode 5, and the corridor economy's currency (salt = hard value, hard truth). The episode is about people paying what they actually owe rather than what's convenient to pay.

## Dara's Cold Open

The outline called for a broker scene from much earlier in the season. This is the payoff. Dara is calibrated, transactional, and has been holding a piece of this for eleven years — her action in this episode is the most proactive thing she does in the whole season, and it happens before anyone tells her to. She sends the envelope because she read the junction list and understood why it was four months early. I wanted her to do this without being told. The eleven-year wait implies exactly this kind of competence.

I kept the Dara scene short and concrete. Her voice is efficient — she doesn't narrate her emotional state, she acts. The detail about having written Brigid's contact code on the envelope eight months ago and not opened it since is meant to show the shape of how she operates: prepare, wait, execute.

## Naila's Debt

This is the emotional center of the episode. I structured it in two parts: the explicit debt (the envelope Adaeze left with her) and the interest (the active misdirection that blew her neutrality). The distinction matters to Naila — she's precise about the difference between what she was asked to do and what she chose to do. The word "choice" in her final speech to Kirin is the only moment in the episode that approaches thematic statement, and I kept it in her voice rather than letting the narration underline it.

The letter from Adaeze (delivered by Naila) is the second sealed letter in the series — the first was at the second way-station on the inbound route. This one is shorter and more personal. The "the key has been in a closet behind thirty years of linens / you found it / that is the whole job" line is the episode's emotional landing point, and I saved it for the rail scene rather than the Salt scene because I wanted the reader to sit with it in transit, not in a charged location.

## Ardent's Mistake

The irony of Ardent filing a compliance flag that puts a human reviewer on Kirin's materials assessment is the episode's structural turn. I wanted to earn it technically: the Compact's review process exists specifically to route flagged accounts to human review rather than automated processing. Ardent used a legitimate tool for its legitimate purpose and created the opposite of what he intended.

His second interlude is where he works out exactly how much he's been missing. Not just Kirin's assessment — the realization that Adaeze had been filing materials assessments through the routine eastern district queue since 2104. He searched for intelligence operations. She ran compliance paperwork. The gap between what he was looking for and what she was doing is the defining irony of his eighteen years on this problem.

I kept his realization compressed and non-melodramatic. He sits with it. He closes an interface. He notes that the door has been closed and the room has been filling with water for twenty-seven years. That's as close as he gets to self-recognition.

## Tomas

The call needed to do two things: show that Ardent has already reached Sable River, and reactivate a relationship the listener hasn't heard from since episode 2. Tomas is scared in a very specific way — he's a person who has known something for a long time and has been carrying it without anyone asking him to carry it, and now someone has asked about it and the weight has become visible. His voice is controlled in a way that Kirin, who has known him for fourteen years, immediately reads as wrong. He doesn't explain what's wrong because he doesn't fully understand it either.

Adaeze's letter's final instruction — "take care of Tomas, he has known about this longer than is good for anyone to know about something" — is the line that recontextualizes the relationship. She knew what she was asking him to carry when she didn't approach him in 2118. She did it anyway. That's not uncomplicated.

## Yusra at Larch

She's a logistics problem, as she has always been, but in this episode she does something unexpected: she volunteers that Larch has independent documentation going back twelve years, filed with the Compact's board annually. This is not warm. It's not friendly. It's a person who has been watching a thing fail for years and who sees an opportunity to make that watching matter. "Tell whoever you're telling that Larch has documentation going back to 2110" — she's not helping Kirin, she's contributing to something she's been preparing to contribute to for twelve years.

This also adds a second corroborating source for the materials assessment filed at Kesmark.

## What I Deliberately Left Unresolved

- The N-4W settlement cluster: Adaeze's letter says someone has been sent. Kirin is trusting that. We don't know who or what they found. This is intentional — it's the one thread Kirin explicitly releases management of.
- The formatted survey: still assembling. Still in motion. The Rootstock Protocol filing remains possible but not yet executable. Ep 13-14 territory.
- Veritas Structured's alternative authentication avenue: they're still out there with a procurement inquiry for Rootstock-compatible hardware. The source of that hardware remains unidentified. Active threat.

## Continuity Flags for Ep 13

- Salt is now a different place: Naila's neutrality is blown. The Curator's operatives will know what she did. Ep 13 ("The Empty Way Back") should show the consequences — the way-station no longer operates the same way.
- Tomas: he's been visited, he's scared, he knows Kirin is coming. Ep 14 territory, but Ep 13 can plant him as a figure waiting at the edge of the city.
- Ardent: he has closed several interfaces. He has nothing left that can reach her within the Compact's administrative framework without becoming visible in ways he has spent eighteen years avoiding. He will need to think about what comes next — and that thinking will probably take him somewhere new in Ep 13-14.
- Berin arriving in Sable River: fifteen months outside, now returning. What the city looks like to him is a character note that could be useful in Ep 14.
- The review queue: the assessment has a 3-5 business day review window. That means Brigid could receive three pieces of documentation (Dara's envelope, the materials assessment review, Kirin in person) within the same window. The convergence timing is the engine of Ep 14.

## What I'm Worried About

The episode is driven by paperwork operations rather than physical confrontation, which is correct for the story but requires the paperwork to feel consequential. I think the Ardent-uses-his-flag-against-himself beat carries enough irony to make the filing feel active rather than procedural. But Ep 13 needs a physical danger moment — the outline calls for "a trap, a real near-fatal moment" — to ensure the back half of the season doesn't become entirely administrative.

The Naila scene is the longest in the episode. I think it earns its length, but it requires Naila to be precise about the distinction between owed debt and chosen interest, which is a distinction that could read as hairsplitting if it's not delivered exactly right in performance.
